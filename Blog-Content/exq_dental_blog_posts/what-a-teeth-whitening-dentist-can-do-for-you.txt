# What a Teeth Whitening Dentist Can do for You

At-home pastes, trays, and gels often change color only a little. If you want a larger, more even change, in-office whitening is usually the more controlled option. A dentist can match the method to your enamel, your sensitivity, and the restorations you already have.

Dr. Alexie Aguil has been treating whitening patients at Exquisite Dentistry in Los Angeles for years. The first visit is a conversation about shade goals, then one of a few paths.

## Zoom whitening

Zoom is an in-office gel-and-light treatment. Teeth are cleaned first so the gel can sit evenly. The gel is applied, then a Zoom light is used to help lift stains. A typical session takes about an hour. Some patients see a change of several shades in that visit; the exact result depends on the starting color and the type of stain. We will tell you if sensitivity is likely and how we manage it.

## Porcelain veneers, when whitening is not enough

Whitening does not change crowns, fillings, or existing veneers. If the goal is a larger change in shape or a stain that will not lift, veneers may be the more honest plan. Porcelain shells can cover color and small chips, usually over three visits: records and a digital impression, a try-in with temporaries, then bonding of the finished veneers. Longevity depends on bite, hygiene, and how the restorations are used. A consultation is the place to see whether whitening, veneers, or both make sense.

## Cleanings still matter

A regular cleaning removes plaque and tartar that sit on top of the enamel. Light surface stain often looks better after that visit alone. Even if you already want in-office whitening, staying on a cleaning schedule is what keeps the new shade from dulling as quickly.
